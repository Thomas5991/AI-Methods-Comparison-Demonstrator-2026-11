# Brief: Energy-Aware Planning & Control Demonstrator (derived from v2 robot demo)

## Goal
Transform the attached demonstrator "AI Architectures in Action: LLMs, JEPA, RL & MPC" from a 2D robot
sandbox into a demonstrator for energy-aware production planning and plant dispatch with energy storage.
Purpose: science communication for researchers / conference audiences (English). It must stay compact,
self-explanatory, and fair. Every architecture shows a characteristic strength and failure mode that
is computed by the simulation, not scripted as a cartoon.

Reuse the existing file structure: header with architecture selector, 2-column workspace
(left: simulation canvas, right: live pipeline, KPIs, "Why is it behaving this way?"), and a
comparison matrix at the bottom. Keep the visual style: Tailwind CDN, light mode, Inter font.

Title: "AI Architectures for Energy-Aware Planning & Control: MPC, LLMs, Learned World Models & RL"
Subtitle: "Interactive digital-twin sandbox: industrial demand-side management and plant dispatch with storage"

## Technical constraints
- One self-contained HTML file, vanilla JS, canvas rendering, Tailwind via CDN, no other network calls.
- Discrete-time simulation: 15-min steps, 24 h day = 96 steps, rolling "now" cursor, auto-loop with reset.
  Default speed: about 45 s per simulated day. Keep Pause / Reset / speed control.
- Behaviours must emerge from the implemented algorithms, using the minimal real implementations below.
- Persistent disclaimer in the footer: "Stylized educational simulation. Controllers are simplified;
  LLM behaviour is emulated (no live model call); the latent world model is conceptual (JEPA-type)."

## Application case switch (toggle in the header): A | B

### A. Industrial demand-side management (PPC)
- Base load 0.8 MW, PV up to 1.0 MW_p (daily curve), grid import limit 1.5 MW, demand charge on the daily peak.
- Day-ahead price curve with morning and evening peaks (about 60–250 €/MWh).
- Battery: 2 MWh / 1 MW, round-trip efficiency 90 %.
- Thermal storage: 3 MWh_th, charged by an e-boiler/heat pump of 0.8 MW_el; process heat demand profile.
- Flexible jobs (Gantt rows): Furnace batch (600 kW, 3 h, deadline 18:00), Paint line (400 kW, 4 h,
  window 06–20), Pre-cooling (300 kW, 2 h, before 14:00), Compressed-air buffer (200 kW, shiftable).
- Decisions: job start times, battery and thermal-storage charge/discharge.
- KPIs: energy cost (€), peak import (kW), deadline misses, limit violations.

### B. Power plant dispatch (CHP plant with district heating)
- CHP unit 50 MW_el / 60 MW_th, min load 40 %, ramp 10 % per 15 min, start-up cost.
- Back-up gas boiler 40 MW_th; heat storage 400 MWh_th; district-heating demand 40–90 MW_th with a morning peak.
- Electricity price curve as in A, scaled.
- Decisions: CHP on/off and setpoint, boiler setpoint, heat-storage charge/discharge.
- KPIs: net margin (€), unmet heat demand (MWh), ramp/min-load violations, starts.

## Architectures (6 selector cards; Pixel-WM is removed)
0. **Classical MPC (MILP + physics twin)**, the baseline. White-box first-principles model with nominal
   parameters. Optimiser: dynamic programming over a discretised state (SoC/storage level × time) plus
   enumeration of job start slots. Label it "MILP-type optimiser (stylized DP solver)".
   Respects the user-set horizon. Reports "solver nodes" as its compute metric; these grow steeply with horizon.
1. **LLM only**: emulated. Produces a schedule from plausible heuristics ("run flexible loads when price
   is low", "charge before the evening peak") with rough quantities. Does NOT enforce energy balance,
   power limits or storage bounds. Parses natural-language rules (see S4) correctly.
2. **Learned latent WM + MPC** (JEPA-type, conceptual): sampling-based MPC (CEM or random shooting,
   about 64 rollouts) over a learned dynamics model. "Learned" is implemented as online
   recursive-least-squares estimation of the true plant parameters (storage capacity, efficiency,
   thermal losses), so it tracks reality under model mismatch. No rule channel. Myopic beyond its horizon.
3. **Hybrid: LLM + latent WM + MPC**: the LLM sets semantic subgoals and constraints (e.g. "SoC ≥ 80 %
   by 16:00", injected rules); MPC executes them on the learned model.
4. **Latent WM + RL + MPC** (TD-MPC2-style): adds a value function V(t, storage level) computed by
   value iteration on the discretised grid, used as terminal cost. Solves the beyond-horizon
   storage problem. The value function is trained on the old rules, so it stays stale after a rule shift.
5. **Full triad: LLM + latent WM + RL + MPC**: as 4, plus LLM constraint injection that overrides the
   stale value function when rules change.

## Scenarios (4 tabs; the same semantics apply to both cases)
**S1 Forecast disturbances**: a price spike, PV dip (A) or heat-demand surge (B) appears during the day.
Users can drag the spike/dip on the timeline. Button "Inject disturbance": machine breakdown (A, the
furnace stops for 1 h) or CHP trip (B, 1 h).

**S2 Model mismatch**: the real plant deviates from the nominal twin. A slider sets the deviation from
0 to 40 % (default 25 %): battery capacity fade and higher thermal losses (A); fouled-boiler efficiency
and higher storage losses (B). The physics twin plans with nominal values, so storage hits its bounds
earlier than planned and the KPIs show violations or deficits. A button "Engineer re-parametrises
twin" fixes the baseline after a visible delay.

**S3 Storage dilemma (beyond-horizon payoff)**: the evening price/demand peak (A) or the morning heat
peak (B) lies beyond the default MPC horizon of 4 h. A horizon slider runs from 1 to 12 h. Charging now
costs money and only pays off later, so myopic controllers never charge (the local optimum,
analogous to the original Key & Gate scenario). The baseline solves it once the horizon reaches about
8–10 h, but its solver nodes and compute time rise visibly. Make this trade-off explicit in the KPI panel.

**S4 Rule shift (zero-shot)**: a natural-language instruction is injected mid-day through a text field
with presets:
- A: "Grid operator: cap import at 1.0 MW between 17:00 and 19:00" / "Furnace maintenance until 14:00"
- B: "Redispatch request: CHP max 30 MW_el 16:00–20:00" / "Heat storage max 70 % (inspection)"
Only the LLM architectures parse it instantly. The baseline needs the "Engineer encodes constraint"
button (with a delay). Architectures without an LLM keep violating the rule.

## Visualisation (left canvas: stacked panels with a shared time axis, 00–24 h, "now" cursor)
1. Price curve, PV forecast (A) or heat demand (B), with a forecast uncertainty band ahead of "now".
   Disturbances are draggable here.
2. Gantt chart: job blocks (A) or unit-commitment bars (B); planned blocks are shown ghosted, executed ones solid.
3. Power/heat balance: stacked load or generation against the grid limit, with the limit line and violations in red.
4. Storage levels: battery SoC and thermal storage. Toggle "Show MPC rollouts" shows the fan of
   candidate trajectories over the horizon. Toggle "Show RL value V(t, SoC)" shows a heatmap behind
   this panel. This is the direct analogue of the original V(s) heatmap.
Rule-constrained windows appear as hatched zones once they are known to the controller, and
as red-outlined zones while they are ignored.
